Bitcoin ETF vs Owning Bitcoin: What Stock Investors Should Know

By Tom Snellman · October 20, 2026 · 5 min read

In short: A Bitcoin ETF puts Bitcoin's price inside your normal brokerage account. Owning Bitcoin directly gives you the asset itself, with full control and full responsibility. Neither is right for everyone, and many investors end up using both.

Why this question matters now

Since US spot Bitcoin ETFs launched in January 2024, you can buy Bitcoin exposure the same way you buy an S&P 500 fund. Canada has had spot Bitcoin ETFs since 2021. So the question comes up all the time: should I just buy the ETF, or own the real thing?

It comes down to five things: convenience, cost, control, taxes and timing. Let's take them one at a time.

What a spot Bitcoin ETF actually is

A spot Bitcoin ETF is a fund that holds real Bitcoin with a custodian. When you buy a share, you own a slice of the fund, not the Bitcoin. The share price tracks Bitcoin, minus a small annual fee.

You trade it like any stock. Same broker, same account, normal market hours.

What owning Bitcoin directly means

You buy Bitcoin on an exchange. Then you either leave it there or move it to a wallet you control. With self-custody you hold the private keys yourself, usually on a hardware wallet, and nobody else can freeze or move your coins.

The flip side is simple. Lose your keys and your recovery phrase, and your Bitcoin is gone. There's no support line to call.

Convenience: the ETF wins

You already have a broker. No new account, no wallet, nothing new to learn. Your Bitcoin shows up next to your other holdings, rebalancing takes one click, and your heirs inherit it like any other security.

For many stock investors, that alone settles it.

Cost: it depends on how long you hold

ETFs charge an expense ratio every year. The cheapest funds are well under half a percent, but you pay it again every year you hold. Buying Bitcoin directly costs a trading fee or spread once. After that, holding is free.

So for a few months the ETF is often cheaper, and over many years owning Bitcoin directly usually wins. Fees change, so check each fund's current fact sheet.

Taxes: the ETF often fits better

In the US and Canada, the ETF's biggest practical advantage is where it can sit. Held in an IRA, Roth IRA or TFSA, your Bitcoin exposure can grow tax-deferred or tax-free. That can matter more than any fee difference.

Directly held crypto is taxed differently, and the rules are still changing. That's a question for your tax advisor, not a blog post.

Control: owning Bitcoin wins

Bitcoin was built so you don't have to trust a bank or a broker. With an ETF, you're trusting the fund and its custodian. For most people that trust is reasonable. It's still trust.

With self-custody, nobody stands between you and your Bitcoin. Some investors care about that a lot. Others never think about it.

Timing: the hidden difference

Bitcoin trades all weekend. ETFs don't.

If Bitcoin drops sharply on a Saturday, ETF holders can't react until Monday morning, often after a price gap. For long-term holders this barely matters. It matters more if you want to act on market cycles, because the weekly close that cycle investors watch happens on Sunday.

So which one should you choose?

The ETF is likely the better fit if you want Bitcoin in your existing brokerage or retirement account and would rather not deal with wallets and keys. It's also the natural choice if tax-advantaged accounts matter to you.

Owning Bitcoin directly is likely the better fit if you plan to hold for many years and want to skip the annual fee. It suits you if independence from intermediaries matters and you're willing to learn self-custody properly.

Plenty of investors do both: the ETF in a retirement account, plus some Bitcoin in self-custody.

The question most people skip

ETF or real Bitcoin is a decision about how to hold. The bigger question is when to buy, and when to take profits.

Bitcoin moves in roughly four-year cycles: long accumulation phases, strong bull markets and deep bear markets. Buy near a cycle top and you can wait years to break even, however you hold it. Where you are in the cycle matters more than which wrapper you pick.
